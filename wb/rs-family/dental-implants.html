What is a dental implant, exactly?

An implant has three parts working together. Tap a part — or the diagram — to see what it does.

The Crown — the visible tooth

The Crown

The visible, tooth-shaped part on top. Custom-made to match the color and shape of your natural teeth.

The Abutment

A small connector piece that attaches the crown securely to the implant post below the gumline.

The Post

A titanium post placed directly into the jawbone, acting as an artificial tooth root. This is what makes implants so stable and long-lasting.

The implant process, start to finish

Tap a step to see timing and what to expect.

Consultation & Planning

We evaluate your jawbone, gums, and overall oral health, often with a digital scan, to map out your treatment plan.

Usually one visit. You'll leave with a written plan covering timing, grafting if needed, and a cost estimate.

Implant Placement

The titanium post is placed into the jawbone under local anesthesia. Most patients describe this step as far more comfortable than expected.

Typically a single appointment. Mild soreness afterward is common and usually managed with over-the-counter pain relievers.

Healing & Osseointegration

Over several months, the post fuses naturally with your jawbone, creating a stable, permanent foundation.

This stage usually takes 3 to 6 months. A temporary restoration can often be worn while you heal.

Restoration

Once healed, we attach the abutment and custom crown, completing your new tooth.

The crown is matched to your surrounding teeth. After this visit, you can chew, speak, and smile as you would with a natural tooth.
